# Driveway cost sanity check (report only)

Owner, 2026-10-11: Grayson's 1.28 ac site went from $382k (engine 9) to $873k (engine 10), about $147 a foot, and
even $382k looked implausible. This checks the quantities behind the cost, not the unit rates: the Settings' rates
stay until the local quote arrives (follow-up 47). Generated by `pnpm dw:cost` (`test/tools/driveway-cost-check.test.ts`)
at engine 10. Nothing in the engine changes.

## What drives the $873k

- **The ordinary road's rock: $510k, 58% of it.** 5,670 of its 6,078 yd³ of cut is priced as rock at $90/yd³. The engine prices a road's whole cut as rock wherever the soil map unit has bedrock shallower than `shallowBedrockCm` (100 cm). Pigeonroost, most of Grayson, is mapped with bedrock at 94 cm, so almost every foot of the route counts as rock, however shallow the cut there.
- **The cut itself is a full bench everywhere:** 12 ft wide, cut wholly into the hillside (W² tanθ / 2 per metre), with no fill. On the 17–27° slopes Grayson's route crosses, a builder would cut half and fill half on most of it, a quarter of the excavation.
- **The switchback benches: $173k** for 12 landings, rock already counted only below the bedrock depth (since PR B).
- **Stone and fabric: $144k**: 8 in of stone over the whole 12 ft for the whole length, and fabric under all of it.

## 3. The ordinary road's cut

The engine's rule (`driveway.ts` finish, from the prototype): every 3 m, a full bench across the road's 12 ft at the
ground's slope there, W² tanθ / 2 m² of cross-section, all of it cut, none filled; θ is the terrain's steepest
slope, not the slope across the road. The rock share is the share of the route's length on soil map units with
bedrock shallower than 100 cm, applied to the whole cut (a 2 ft cut on such a unit is all rock).

Slopes along the $873k route: 88 samples under 15°, 183 at 15–20°, 192 at 20–25°, 125 at 25–30°, 16 at 30° or more (of 604, every 3 m).

**Checked by hand at the median sample:** slope 20.7°, tan 0.379. Cross-section 3.7² × 0.379 / 2 = 2.54 m²; over 3 m, 7.61 m³ = 9.96 yd³. The cut is 1.39 m deep at the hillside edge, 0 at the outer edge. Bedrock there: 0.94 m, so 10% of the cut lies below it; the engine prices all of it as rock. As a balanced half bench (cut 1.8 m, fill the rest), 0.63 m² (a quarter), 0.69 m deep, 0% below bedrock.

## 4. Quantity corrections to consider

Each applied on top of the ones above it, unit rates unchanged. For your decision; none is in the engine.

1. **Rock only below the bedrock depth.** The road's cut is a triangle, deepest at the hillside edge; only the part deeper than the soils' depth to bedrock (`brockdepmin`, inside the parcel) is rock, as the benches already do. Outside the parcel the soils aren't queried, so it's soil. (Also fixes the engine's rock share topping 100%: on Macks #2 the rock was 4 yd³ more than the whole cut.)
2. **A balanced half bench on side slopes under 30°.** Cut half the width and fill the other half with it, as a builder would on moderate slopes: a quarter of the excavation. Above 30° fill won't stand, so a full bench stays.
3. **Stone 6 in deep, not 8 in** (4 in of base and 2 in of surface is a common gravel drive).
4. **Fabric only on bottomland** (NRCS's flooded or wet units): on shallow rocky soils it isn't needed.
5. **Not applied, and the other way: relief culverts.** The engine counts a culvert where the route crosses a drainage of 2 ha or more, plus one. A long climbing road also needs cross-drains to take water off it, commonly one every 200–400 ft on grades over 5%: about 15–30 on Grayson's 5,940 ft route, $50–100k at the $3,500 rate, against today's 2 ($7k). Worth a quote line of its own.

## 7. What a builder would build at Grayson's 1.28 ac site

Probably not the 10% road. The site is 338 ft above Mud Creek Ln on a 17–27° hillside. Held to 10–11% it needs about 6,000 ft and 12 switchback landings. Allowed 15% with the steep parts paved, it's 2,888 ft and 5 switchbacks, about $117k of gravel work corrected plus paving on roughly 2,200 ft (TBD): the alternative to price with a builder, if the county allows 15% (follow-up 46 checks Grayson's limit). Through the neighbours at 12% it's 4,160 ft with 10 switchbacks and an easement. The screen should say so: the six-figure 10% cost is real arithmetic on an alignment nobody would choose.
